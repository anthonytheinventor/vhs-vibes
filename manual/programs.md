## Home Recording
The default. A family tape in good shape: gentle wobble, some hiss, a level control that rides a little.

## Fresh Rental
Friday night, new release. Clean, a trace of breathing, loses lock once in a long while.

## 3rd Gen Copy
A copy of a copy of a copy: dull, clipped highs, deep wobble, plenty of noise.

## Vaporwave Mall
Slow warble and a wide chorus under dimmed lights.

## Camcorder 89
Built-in microphone, level control squashing everything, noise recorded with the picture.

## Tracking Error
Buzz on the music, static, and the sound dropping to the backup track.

## Late Night TV
Mono, narrow, hum from the studio.

## Clean Widener
No tape at all: only the chorus, used as a stereo widener.

## Training Video
Please watch carefully. Mono, hiss that swells in every pause, slow warble, head buzz.

## Chewed Tape
The machine ate it. Lurching pitch, dropouts, sound that comes and goes.

## Answer Machine
You have one new message. Telephone tone, narrow and squashed.

## Dream Sequence
Wide, slow and hazy, with hardly any noise.

## Subtle Glue
Just a little tape: gentle clipping, the faintest wobble, no noise.

## Bad Tracking
A good tape in a deck that cannot quite follow it.

## Six Hour Tape
Recorded at the slowest speed: about 100 Hz to 6 kHz, more hiss, more wobble.

## Wedding Video
A camcorder at the back of the hall; the level control rides everything.

## Hi-Fi Master
The best VHS could do: wide, steady, quiet, breathing a little.

## Edge Damage
The top edge of the tape is creased: the sound comes and goes.

## Bootleg Concert
Taped from the crowd on a boombox and copied a few times.

## Public Access
Local cable studio: hum, buzz, and a level control doing its best.

## Init
A blank tape. Everything neutral; the sound passes through unchanged.

## Sticky Tape
Gone sticky with age: it drags and scrapes through the machine.

## Tube TV
The living-room set: a small speaker, mains hum and the whistle of the picture tube.

## Worn Heads
A good Hi-Fi deck with tired heads: buzz that rides on the music and sinks in the pauses, the right channel crackling.

## Dirty Heads
Oxide on the heads: dull, and the highs keep falling away.

## Stereo Rental
An early stereo rental with noise reduction on both sides, and a tape that has aged in between: dull, the highs ducking in the quiet parts.

## Wrong Switch
Recorded with noise reduction, played without: bright, airy, a little squashed, all of the hiss.

## SP Tape
The VHS tape model at standard speed, recorded a little hot: loud parts lose their top and soften, quiet parts stay clear.

## EP Tape
The VHS tape model at the six-hour speed: the highs give way three times sooner and the top of the band is gone.

## VHS - 3rd Gen Copy
3rd Gen Copy on the tape model: every pass recorded hot on a dubbing deck with too much bias. The loud parts go soft and the top is gone.

## VHS - Training Video
Training Video on the tape model: the linear track of a worn SP tape, recorded hot, dull from too much bias.

## VHS - Six Hour Tape
Six Hour Tape on the tape model: EP speed, with the wear, hiss and wobble that EP Tape leaves out.

## VHS - Sticky Tape
Sticky Tape on the tape model: too little bias, so it is brighter and grittier as it drags.

## VHS - Dirty Heads
Dirty Heads on the tape model: too much bias, so the highs give way sooner still.

## Mic - Camcorder 89
Camcorder 89 on the modeled microphone: the camera's level control squashing everything and the deck's AGC after it, a little crunch on the loud parts.

## Mic - Answer Machine
Answer Machine on the modeled microphone: a carbon handset and a phone line, the quiet words sinking.

## Mic - Wedding Video
Wedding Video on the modeled microphone: the camera's level control rides everything, and the deck's AGC rides it again.

## Mic - Bootleg Concert
Bootleg Concert on the modeled microphone: the radio cassette's level control holds the crowd at one level, and the hiss climbs in every pause.

## Mic - Tube TV
Tube TV on the modeled microphone: the boombox held up to the set, its level control lifting the hiss in every pause.

## Hi-Fi - Clean Deck
The Hi-Fi track on a good deck with a new tape: only the FM channel's own faint hiss, at a real deck's level, and no wobble.

## Hi-Fi - Hot Signal
Recorded 25 dB over, through a hotter, grittier input: every loud hit and held loud note flattens against the Hi-Fi track's hard ceiling.

## Hi-Fi - Worn Rental
A tape that has been through many machines: the signal 12 dB down, a steady crackle of clicks, loud head switching, noise that swells with the music, and a moment on the hissy linear track every few seconds.

## Hi-Fi - Tracking Lost
The tracking knob is well off: a hard buzz at every head switch, bursts of clicks at the dips, and about once a second the dull, hissy mono linear track takes over.

## Hi-Fi - Wrong Deck
Recorded 10 dB hot on one deck, played on another whose playback side disagrees: quiet passages come well up, loud ones do not, and it pumps.

## VHS - Borrowed Tape
SP Tape recorded on another deck: the head is 6.5' out of line, so the highs from about 8 kHz up dull and swish gently.

## VHS - Wrong Deck EP
VHS - Six Hour Tape on the wrong deck: 7.5' out of line at EP puts the first notch at 5.1 kHz. It hollows out the top, with a faint ghost of highs above it, and it comes and goes.

## VHS - Factory Aligned
Hysteresis on, with the bias right: an SP tape on a deck set up at the factory, recorded a little under reference level. Close to SP Tape.

## VHS - Bias Drift
Hysteresis on, the bias drifted a third of the way down: a brighter top and a little grit on the quiet parts.

## VHS - Starved Bias
Hysteresis on, the bias near the bottom: soft passages break up, loud ones come through.

## VHS - Whisper Gate
Hysteresis on, almost no bias: a quiet tone hardly records at all, so tails and reverb break off, leaving only the hiss.

## VHS - Hot And Starved
Hysteresis on, too little bias and recorded hot: gritty quiet parts and squashed peaks.

## VHS - Six Hour Starved
VHS - Six Hour Tape with Hysteresis on and too little bias: at EP the highs dull themselves as well.

## NR - Fuzz In The Gaps
Wrong Switch with less hiss and NR Clipper at 80 %: a bright fuzz that swells in the gaps and ducks under the loud hits.

## NR - Blown Encoder
NR Clipper at 100 %, recorded with noise reduction into a hot VHS tape: the harshest pairing.
